# Vendor Inventory and Classification

## Purpose

This document demonstrates how organizations identify third-party relationships and classify vendors based on the level of cybersecurity risk they introduce.

Rather than treating all vendors equally, this approach prioritizes vendors according to business criticality, data exposure, and operational dependency. This classification drives the depth of due diligence, monitoring requirements, and escalation paths.

---

## Vendor Identification

Vendors are identified through collaboration with procurement, IT, business units, and security teams. Typical vendor categories include:

- Cloud service providers
- Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platforms
- Managed service providers (MSPs)
- Telecommunications and infrastructure providers
- Data processors and analytics vendors
- Industrial or operational technology suppliers

Each vendor relationship is documented with basic contextual information, including service provided, system access, and data involvement.

---

## Vendor Classification Criteria

Vendors are classified using the following risk dimensions:

### 1. Business Criticality
- Impact to operations if the vendor becomes unavailable
- Dependency on the vendor for core business processes
- Ability to substitute or replace the vendor

### 2. Data Sensitivity
- Type of data accessed or processed (e.g., personal, financial, operational)
- Volume of data involved
- Regulatory or contractual obligations associated with the data

### 3. Technical Access
- Level of network or system access
- Privileged or administrative access
- Integration with internal systems or environments

### 4. Operational Impact
- Potential impact on safety, availability, or regulatory compliance
- Relevance to OT / ICS environments where applicable

---

## Vendor Risk Tiers (Example)

| Tier | Description | Example Characteristics |
|-----|------------|-------------------------|
| Tier 1 (High Risk) | Critical vendors with significant access or impact | Processes sensitive data, privileged access, high business dependency |
| Tier 2 (Medium Risk) | Important vendors with limited access or data exposure | Limited system access, moderate data sensitivity |
| Tier 3 (Low Risk) | Vendors with minimal impact | No system access, public or non-sensitive data |

Tier assignment determines the depth of risk assessment, due diligence requirements, and review frequency.

---

## Governance Considerations

- Vendor classification is reviewed periodically and when service scope changes
- Business owners remain accountable for vendor relationships
- Security and GRC teams provide risk analysis and recommendations
- Final risk acceptance decisions are made at the appropriate management level

Documenting vendor classification ensures that third-party risk decisions are explicit, traceable, and defensible.

---

## Key Takeaway

Not all vendors represent equal risk.  
A structured vendor inventory and classification process allows organizations to focus risk management efforts where they matter most, while maintaining transparency and accountability.

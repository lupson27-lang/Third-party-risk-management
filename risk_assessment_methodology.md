# Third-Party Risk Assessment Methodology

## Purpose

This document outlines a structured approach for assessing cybersecurity risk introduced by third-party vendors.

The goal is not to eliminate all risk, but to identify, prioritize, and manage third-party risks in a way that aligns with business objectives, regulatory expectations, and risk appetite.

---

## Assessment Triggers

Third-party risk assessments are initiated under the following conditions:

- Onboarding of a new vendor
- Renewal or contract modification
- Change in service scope or data access
- Security incidents involving the vendor
- Periodic reassessment based on risk tier

---

## Risk Assessment Scope

The scope of assessment is determined by vendor risk tier and may include:

- Information security governance and policies
- Data protection and privacy practices
- Access controls and identity management
- Incident detection and response capabilities
- Business continuity and disaster recovery
- Compliance with applicable regulations or standards

High-risk vendors require deeper assessment and validation.

---

## Risk Evaluation Approach

Risk is evaluated using a likelihood and impact model:

### Likelihood Factors
- Security maturity and control environment
- Exposure to threat activity
- History of security incidents
- Complexity of system integration

### Impact Factors
- Business disruption potential
- Data sensitivity and volume
- Regulatory or legal consequences
- Reputational damage

---

## Risk Scoring (Conceptual Example)

| Risk Level | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Low | Minimal business or security impact |
| Medium | Manageable risk requiring monitoring |
| High | Significant risk requiring mitigation or escalation |

Risk scoring informs remediation requirements and risk acceptance decisions.

---

## Governance and Oversight

- Risk assessments are documented and reviewed
- Security and GRC teams provide risk analysis
- Business owners remain accountable for vendor risk
- Senior management approves risk acceptance when required

All decisions are recorded to ensure auditability and traceability.

---

## Key Takeaway

Effective third-party risk management depends on consistent, defensible risk assessments that support informed business decisions — not checkbox compliance.

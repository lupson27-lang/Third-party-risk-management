# Due Diligence and Controls Review

## Purpose

This document describes how third-party vendors are evaluated through due diligence activities and security controls review.

The objective is to validate that vendors have appropriate safeguards in place to manage identified risks — not to assume compliance based on certifications alone.

---

## Due Diligence Approach

Due diligence activities are risk-tier driven and may include:

- Security questionnaires
- Policy and procedure reviews
- Evidence validation
- Independent attestations (e.g., ISO 27001, SOC 2)
- Targeted interviews or clarification requests

Higher-risk vendors require deeper validation and supporting evidence.

---

## Security Control Domains Reviewed

Controls are assessed across key security domains, including:

- Governance and security policies
- Identity and access management
- Data protection and encryption
- Logging, monitoring, and incident response
- Vulnerability and patch management
- Business continuity and disaster recovery

The depth of review depends on the vendor’s access level and risk classification.

---

## Evidence Types

Examples of acceptable evidence include:

- Information security policies and standards
- Incident response and escalation procedures
- Vulnerability management reports
- Access control documentation
- Audit reports or certifications
- Business continuity test results

Evidence is reviewed for relevance, currency, and completeness.

---

## Control Gaps and Findings

Identified gaps are documented with:

- Description of the control weakness
- Associated risk and impact
- Recommended remediation
- Target timelines
- Responsible parties

Not all gaps require remediation if the residual risk is acceptable.

---

## Risk-Based Decision Making

Findings may result in:

- Risk acceptance
- Compensating controls
- Contractual safeguards
- Remediation plans
- Vendor rejection or limitation of scope

Decisions are aligned with organizational risk appetite and business needs.

---

## Key Takeaway

Due diligence is not about eliminating vendor risk — it is about understanding control effectiveness and making informed, defensible risk decisions.
